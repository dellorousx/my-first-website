The challenge

New customers had to move through multiple steps before becoming active users. The experience needed to communicate requirements earlier and make progress feel more predictable.

1Too much information was presented at once.
2Users had limited visibility into what was required next.
3Validation and error states created avoidable friction.

The approach

I mapped the end-to-end journey, identified high-friction moments and worked with product and engineering to simplify the information architecture before moving into detailed UI.

→Clarified requirements before users entered each step.
→Reduced cognitive load through progressive disclosure.
→Designed clearer feedback for validation and recovery.
Outcome

Improved the account activation flow, contributing to higher first-time completion and a clearer path from sign-up to active banking.

Design principles

For a credit product, “easy to use” should not mean hiding important information. The interface makes cost, installment and repayment information understandable before commitment.

Key decisions

01Expose the important numbers at the moment they matter.
02Use consistent components to make the flow feel familiar.
03Separate promotional messaging from financial disclosures.
Design outcome

A more transparent PayLater journey that balances commercial messaging with customer confidence.

Problem framing

Splitting a bill is technically a payment task, but emotionally it is a coordination task. The experience needs to make amounts and responsibilities obvious without creating awkwardness between friends.

Solution direction

Introduce the shared expense first, make each participant’s amount explicit, then provide a lightweight request-and-confirmation loop.

Core insight

The best payment UX can disappear into the social moment it enables.
